It sounds like your friend may be acting condescending or dismissive toward people they are not close to—especially around ordinary, everyday activities. That behavior is usually more about **their own insecurity or need to feel important** than a real measure of other people’s worth. Looking down on others can be a form of downward social comparison: putting someone else “below” oneself to feel safer or superior. [note](https://note.com/no_mouth/n/n300707da8fd7?hl=en)

## Possible reasons

- **Insecurity or low confidence:** They may feel unsure of their own value, so they try to gain a sense of status by criticizing or belittling others. [note](https://note.com/cheeky_liger9981/n/ncba5be3e3e5c?hl=en)
- **Need for approval or belonging:** Acting “better than” certain people can be an attempt to impress a group or seem more interesting, cool, or exclusive.
- **Habit learned from others:** Some people grow up around family or friends who judge people by popularity, appearance, money, hobbies, or social status, and repeat it without realizing.
- **Misreading normal behavior:** Sometimes what looks like contempt is actually awkwardness, social anxiety, or a habit of keeping distance from people they do not know well. People can also misinterpret others’ actions when they feel insecure. [verywellmind](https://www.verywellmind.com/the-liking-gap-12134210)
- **Poor empathy or maturity:** They may not yet understand that ordinary activities—hanging out, playing games, talking casually, having simple routines—do not make someone lesser.

## What it does *not* mean

It does **not** mean the people they look down on are actually boring, worthless, or beneath them. Someone can enjoy simple activities and still be kind, talented, thoughtful, or socially valuable. Your friend’s judgment says more about their attitude than about the other person.

## How you might respond

- Do not feel pressured to join in when they mock or dismiss someone.
- Try a calm boundary: “They’re just doing their own thing; it doesn’t bother me.”
- If it becomes repeated bullying or cruel gossip, name it clearly: “That feels unnecessarily mean.”
- Notice whether this is a pattern. If they regularly put people down, it may affect how safe or respected you feel around them.

If this friend makes you feel like you also have to prove yourself or look down on others to be accepted, that is a warning sign. A good friend should not require you to participate in putting people down.
